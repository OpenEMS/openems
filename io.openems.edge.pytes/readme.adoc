= Pytes JS3 Hybrid Inverter

Driver for the Pytes JS3 hybrid inverter (Modbus TCP, RS485 protocol Ver 3.2 register map,
input registers 33xxx, holding registers 43xxx/44xxx). All components share one Modbus
bridge and unit id; the inverter accepts only **one** TCP connection at a time.

== Components

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
|Factory PID |Natures |Description

|`Pytes.Hybrid.ESS`
|`ManagedSymmetricEss`, `HybridEss`
|The inverter itself. Receives the OpenEMS AC set-point, owns the work state machine
and the remote dispatch registers 44100–44108, does the dynamic feed-in limitation.
Battery and DC charger register themselves at the ESS (`addBattery()` / `addCharger()`).

|`Pytes.Battery`
|`Battery`
|Battery values from the inverter's battery port and the BMS (regs 33133–33150):
voltage, current, SoC, BMS current limits, fault words. `DcDischargePower` is
calculated from BMS current × voltage × direction. Also reads the backup port load
(reg 33148), because it sits in the same register block.

|`Pytes.Hybrid.DcCharger`
|`EssDcCharger`
|PV strings 1–4 and total PV power (regs 33049–33058), energy counters.

|`Meter.Pytes.Grid`
|`ElectricityMeter` (GRID)
|The inverter's own grid meter at the grid connection point (regs 33251 ff.).

|`Meter.Pytes.Backup`
|`ElectricityMeter` (CONSUMPTION_METERED)
|Loads on the inverter's backup port (regs 33137 ff., total in 33148).
|===

== How the AC set-point is applied

OpenEMS controllers never ask for "everything the PV has": the power solver resolves all
constraints (Balancing "grid = 0", a limiter's "AC >= PV", ...) into exactly **one AC
set-point per cycle** for the whole inverter output (`ActivePower`, reg 33079, *including*
PV). The ESS config `essSetpoint` selects what that number means for the inverter
(regs 44105/44106). The choice decides how the inverter behaves whenever the PV cannot be
absorbed by the battery - which is most of a sunny day.

=== `BATTERY_CONTROL` (44105 = 2, default, recommended) - "charge/discharge the battery with N watts"

What is written::
The EMS computes the battery power `set-point − PV`, adds a feed-forward for the inverter's
constant bias (~190–220 W towards charging) and the conversion losses (~30 W + 3 % of
battery + PV throughput), plus a slow integral trim (separate for charge and discharge),
clamps it to the raw BMS/config limits and writes it to 44106 (negative = discharge).

What the inverter does::
It regulates the **battery** to that value and lets the PV pass through: AC output = PV +
battery, with the MPPT always at the maximum power point. PV that the battery does not take
is exported without anybody having to know how much PV is available.

Effects::
* "Battery 0 W" means "export everything the PV produces". A limiter's "do not charge" or a
  full battery therefore never curtails PV; there is no deadlock (see below).
* A cloud reduces the AC output immediately; the battery keeps its commanded power. No
  battery transients at cloud edges.
* Battery limits from OpenEMS are enforced directly (the value is clamped DC-side).
* Grid feed-in limitation: the inverter's own export cap (backstop) and the dynamic AC
  output cap (reg 43052) work as described below; the battery set-point stays in force
  below the cap.
* Accuracy at the grid point ±50–100 W (bias/loss model plus trim), set-point reached after
  ~10 s. At a set-point of 0 the inverter idles at ~30 W charging (its bias); the trim is
  intentionally off below 50 W so it does not chase that. Backup-port loads need no special
  handling: the total AC is what is controlled.

Use it when the intent is battery-related: self-consumption with a limiter, "do not charge
above x %", PV pass-through, robust operation on days with broken clouds. This is the
default and the recommended mode: measured on 2026-09-19 over a full cycle (charging at
the limit, taper at 82-85 %, stop at 85 %, PV exported and - with the inverter's export cap
- curtailed) it did exactly what the controllers asked for, without any of the workarounds
the AC mode needs.

=== `AC_OUTPUT_CONTROL` (44105 = 4) - "deliver N watts at the AC output"

What is written::
The set-point is written directly (register in 10 W steps, minus the backup-port load, see
below). No feed-forward, the trim stays ~0.

What the inverter does::
It holds the **AC output** at that value and decides the PV/battery split itself: PV first,
the battery covers what is missing, and PV above `set-point + what the battery takes` is
**curtailed** by moving the MPPT off the maximum power point (string voltage rises towards
open circuit, e.g. 530–545 V instead of ~470 V). Nothing in the register map reports this
curtailment; the measured PV simply equals what is used.

Effects::
* Accuracy at the grid point ±20 W, reached after ~10 s, no overshoot (measured
  2026-09-16).
* It regulates only the **grid-side AC port**; loads on the **backup port** are supplied on
  top. The register value is therefore `set-point − backup load` (reg 33148).
* It does **not** know the OpenEMS battery limits (it charged with 55 A against a 40 A
  config / 50 A BMS limit). `AllowedChargePower` / `AllowedDischargePower` are reported
  AC-side (`limit ± PV`) and the set-point is clamped to
  `[PV − charge limit, PV + discharge limit − losses]` regardless of what a controller asks
  for.
* **The measured PV is not the available PV.** Once the battery is full (or the inverter
  refuses to charge, e.g. above its own max-charge SoC), a set-point at house consumption
  curtails the PV to house consumption, the next cycle's constraints (`AC >= measured PV`)
  are computed from that curtailed value, and the loop locks: live on 2026-09-18, 6 kW of
  available PV were used at 100 W for as long as nobody raised the set-point. Whether the
  operating point drifts up or down depends on ±100 W of battery noise. The ESS therefore
  runs a *surplus search* in this mode (see feed-in limitation) that keeps probing for
  more PV - a workaround, not a fix.
* A cloud does not change the AC output: the inverter takes the missing power from the
  battery until the EMS lowers the set-point (measured PV lags one cycle, the inverter
  follows a new set-point after ~5 s). Expect 5–15 s of battery discharge up to the size of
  the PV drop at every cloud edge (~3 Wh per event, harmless but visible).
* With the backstop export cap active the inverter ignores the set-point completely (see
  feed-in limitation).

Use it only when the grid point has to be exact: peak shaving, zero export as a hard
target, tariff-driven grid charging. Not for plain self-consumption: with a full battery on
a sunny day it locks the PV at house consumption (see above).

=== `GRID_POINT_CONTROL` (44105 = 3)

The inverter regulates the grid connection point with its own meter. Not usable together
with OpenEMS controllers (two controllers on the same grid point).

=== Common to all modes

Register 44106 is in steps of 10 W. The whole block 44100–44108 is re-written every cycle as
one FC16 frame: the inverter does not persist these registers and falls back to its own
self-use mode after the failsafe timeout (44101, 5 min) without writes. The same happens
while the ESS is not in work state NORMAL (e.g. the first 2–3 cycles after start). In its
own self-use mode the inverter exports every PV surplus itself, limited only by its export
cap - that is the only "give everything you have" the inverter knows.

== Values the inverter reports

* `ess0/DcDischargePower` is derived as `ActivePower − PV` (the AC-side battery
  contribution, what Sum, UI and all OpenEMS controllers use). The measured DC value is
  `battery0/DcDischargePower`; the DC energy counters use the measured value.
* `ActivePower` follows a set-point change after ~5 s, the BMS values after ~11 s.
* The inverter's own battery power reading (reg 33149) and the BMS calculation agree within a
  few watts.
* `getSurplusPower()` = PV + raw DC charge limit (for `Controller.Ess.Hybrid.SurplusFeedToGrid`);
  null while the dynamic feed-in limitation curtails PV (the measured PV is not the available one).

== Battery current limits

Four sources exist; the smallest value wins in `AllowedChargeDischargeHandler`
(measured 2026-09-17):

* EMS config (`Pytes.Battery` maxChargeCurrent / maxDischargeCurrent) - enforced by
  the EMS via the set-point clamps; only effective while the inverter follows the
  EMS set-point.
* BMS request (regs 33143/33144, "From BMS") - a request to the inverter; the BMS
  itself only protects with its hard thresholds (fault words 33145/33146).
* Inverter storage-control setting (regs 43117/43118) - the app's "max. charge /
  discharge current", 1:1. **This is the limit the inverter actually uses** when it
  runs autonomously (see below). It is read, never written.
* Inverter battery-model setting (regs 43012/43013) - 999.0 A placeholder with
  communicating lithium batteries; ignored when 0 or above 150 A.

The inverter exceeds its own limit by ~10 % for a while when the AC set-point asks
for more than the battery can deliver, so keep the EMS config at or above the
inverter setting - a stricter EMS value cannot be enforced in autonomous mode.

== Grid feed-in limitation (e.g. 70 % rule)

The limit is configured centrally in `Core.Meta` (`gridFeedInLimitationType =
DYNAMIC_LIMITATION`, `maximumGridFeedInLimit` in W). Controllers such as
`Controller.Ess.GridOptimizedCharge` keep the export below the limit by charging the battery
as long as the battery can take the surplus. Two mechanisms in the ESS handle the rest:

Dynamic limitation (`meter_id`)::
When the export at the grid meter exceeds the Meta limit, the ESS caps the inverter's AC
output (reg 43052, "limited power" in % of the rated power) at
`consumption + limit - tolerance` (tolerance 500 W, at most half of the limit; the cap is never below the consumption), where consumption = ESS AC power + grid power (house incl.
backup port). The inverter then curtails PV; the EMS set-point (battery charge/discharge)
stays in force below the cap. Grid and ESS power are averaged over 5 cycles, the register is
written only on change and at most every 10 s. The cap is released (100 %) when it would
exceed the rated power or when the inverter outputs clearly less than the cap it applies
(PV dropped), evaluated 10 s after the last write. Channels: `PvLimitActive` (INFO),
`AcOutputLimit` (W), read-back `LimitedPowerSetting` / `LimitedPowerActualValue`.
The inverter reports the active cap in reg 33121 (`OperatingStatLimitedExternal`, INFO).

Surplus search (`AC_OUTPUT_CONTROL` only)::
Because the inverter curtails PV to the set-point in this mode without reporting it, the
ESS raises an AC floor above the measured PV in steps (300 W, doubled while the PV follows,
max. 1200 W, one step per 10 s) whenever the battery does not absorb more (charging at its
limit, or idle). If the battery has to fill the gap instead (ActivePower − PV rises by more
than 150 W against its average at the step), the available PV is reached: the floor falls
back to the level the inverter delivered before and the search pauses (60 s, doubled on
every consecutive hit, max. 5 min). A PV drop during a step (cloud) is not counted as a
hit. Steps are only taken while the gap is steady over 5 cycles (after a restart the AC
output lags the PV by hundreds of watts). The floor is bounded by
`consumption + Meta limit − 300 W` so that the inverter's export cap does not engage (it
would ignore the set-point), never exceeds the measured PV outside a step, and is off at
night and while reg 43052 limits. Channel: `SurplusFloor` (W, 0 = off). Not needed in
`BATTERY_CONTROL`, where PV passes through by itself.

Hardware backstop (`feedPowerEnable = ENABLE`)::
Additionally writes the hard limit from Meta (and a ripple control receiver, if present)
into the inverter (reg 44102 export limit switch, reg 44104 limit in 100 W steps). The
inverter then limits the export at its grid meter itself. Channels: `GridFeedInLimit`
(applied value), `GridFeedInLimitMismatch` (warning when the read-back differs for more than
30 s). The dynamic limitation aims below the limit, so the backstop only acts during its ~5 s
reaction time - with a small limit the backstop is usually first. Measured 2026-09-19
(BATTERY_CONTROL, limit 400 W, SoC 85 %, limiter blocks charging): the backstop held the
export at ~365 W, curtailed the PV and **respected the battery set-point of 0 W** (the
set-point is ignored only in AC_OUTPUT_CONTROL).

Measured behaviour with the backstop active (2026-09-17, limit 400 W, PV 5.9 kW): the
inverter keeps the export exactly at the limit, **ignores the AC set-point** and runs
autonomously in this order: export up to the limit -> charge the battery up to its own
setting (regs 43117/43118, exceeded by ~10 %) -> curtail PV. EMS-side battery limits and
OpenEMS controllers (Balancing etc.) have no effect while the backstop is active; this is
why the dynamic limitation via reg 43052 exists (measured: 43052 caps the AC output exactly,
20 % = 3000 W of 15 kVA).

== Settings that are not touched

The SoC limits (regs 43010/43011/43018) and the backup port setting (reg 43111) configured
in the inverter are authoritative; `setDefaultValues()` exists but is intentionally not
called.

== Modbus load

Every frame costs ~35 ms plus ~2.3 ms per register on this inverter. The bundle uses
5 HIGH-priority read tasks, one FC16 write and one LOW task per cycle (~560 ms of a 1 s
cycle). Changing the bridge configuration (e.g. log verbosity) forces a reconnect and the
inverter refuses new connections for ~60 s until the old session is released.
