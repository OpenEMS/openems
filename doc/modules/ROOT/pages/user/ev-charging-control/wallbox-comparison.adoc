__As of: 2026-11-01 · OpenEMS version 2026.11.0-SNAPSHOT (development version, branch develop)__

This page compares how **OpenEMS**, **evcc** and **openWB 2** charge electric cars: which wallboxes they control, what charging modes there are, how they take the home storage into account and how load management works. The basis in each case is the source code (OpenEMS develop, evcc development version “master”, openWB 2 “core” master). None of this has been tried out. How the three systems exchange data **with each other** is on xref:user/third-party/overview.adoc[Third-party systems on OpenEMS].

Note on evcc: In the development version and in the published version 0.318.2 (latest version on 2026-11-01), the previous modes “PV” and “Min{plus}PV” are combined into one mode **“Smart”**. The old names are still accepted and converted to “Smart”.

[#sec-supported-wallboxes]
== Supported wallboxes

[#sec-openems]
=== OpenEMS

OpenEMS has two charge point connections side by side:

[cols="3*",options="header"]
|===
| Connection
| Wallboxes in the code
| Control

| **New charge point control (EVSE)**
| Alfen, Alpitronic, Ambibox, Bender, Hardy Barth (cPH1 and others), Heidelberg Connect, KEBA (Modbus, UDP, Core), Mennekes, Voltie
| xref:user/ev-charging-control/surplus-charging.adoc[Surplus charging and phase switching], cluster, time schedule, energy optimizer
| **Older connection (EVCS)**
| ABL, Alpitronic Hypercharger, go-e (HTTP, Modbus, Charger Home), Hardy Barth, Heidelberg Energy, KEBA (KeContact, P40), Mennekes, Spelsberg SMART, Webasto Next and Unite, OCPP charging stations (server; drivers for ABL and IES Keywatt), openWB (read only)
| Control function “Controller Electric Vehicle Charging Station”, multi-charge-point management
|===

Some manufacturers exist in both connections. New apps in the app center use EVSE control for Alfen, KEBA and Mennekes (see xref:user/devices/cluster.adoc[Cluster]).

[#sec-evcc]
=== evcc

evcc comes with over 200 templates for “chargers” from around 185 brands, including almost all common wallboxes (ABL, Alfen, Easee, go-e, Hardy Barth, Heidelberg, KEBA, Mennekes, openWB, Tesla, Webasto, Zaptec and many more), general routes such as OCPP, EEBus and Modbus as well as switchable sockets and heat pumps or heating elements. For OpenEMS systems, there is **no** template that controls a wallbox connected to OpenEMS via OpenEMS; evcc always controls the wallbox directly.

[#sec-openwb]
=== openWB

openWB 2 controls charge points via these modules:

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| Module
| What

| Internal openWB
| the built-in charge point (also Duo with two charge points)
| Secondary openWB, openWB series2 satellit, openWB Pro
| other openWB devices, controlled by a central openWB
| openWB adapter for DC chargers
| DC chargers via an openWB adapter
| smartWB / EVSE-WiFi
| only third-party manufacturer with its own module
| MQTT charge point
| general charge point: openWB writes target current and phases via MQTT, an external program must pass this on to the actual wallbox
|===

openWB therefore essentially controls its own hardware. Third-party wallboxes can only be integrated via smartWB or via the MQTT charge point with a self-built connection.

[#sec-charging-modes-in-detail]
== Charging modes in detail

[#sec-openems-evse-control]
=== OpenEMS (EVSE control)

* **No Charge**, **PV Surplus with guaranteed charge** (always at least at the lowest power, surplus on top), **PV Surplus**, **Fast Charging**.
* Mode fixed, via time schedule or once until unplugged (“One-Shot”). Optional energy limit per charging session.
* **Smart**: Time schedule entries with minimum charging amount; the energy optimizer then plans (see xref:user/schedule/overview.adoc[Schedule], xref:user/storage-control/time-of-use-tariffs.adoc[Time-of-use tariffs]).
* Start after 10 to 20 seconds of sufficient surplus, pause only after about one minute without surplus, then 5 minutes lock time; details on xref:user/ev-charging-control/surplus-charging.adoc[Surplus charging].

The older connection only has “Force charging” (with minimum power, default 7,360 W per phase) and “Optimized charging” (with optional minimum power), plus the priority car or storage (see below). Change between charging and pause after 120 or 30 seconds at the earliest.

[#sec-evcc-2]
=== evcc

* **Off**, **Now** (immediately at full power) and **Smart**.
* **Smart** charges with PV surplus; with “always charge” (permanently or once until unplugged), charging takes place at least at minimum current. With a low electricity price below a limit, Smart charges fully (“smart charging”); with high feed-in remuneration it can reduce charging. An optimizer can plan start, stop and power itself.
* **Charging plan**: target SoC or energy amount by a time; evcc selects the cheapest times.
* **Minimum SoC**: up to this vehicle SoC, fast charging always takes place; **charging limit** as SoC or energy.
* Switching on and off with thresholds and delay (default 1 minute to switch on, 3 minutes to switch off).
* Vehicle SoC comes from the vehicle cloud or the wallbox; without SoC, evcc works with energy.

[#sec-openwb-2]
=== openWB

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| Mode
| Behavior

| Instant charging
| immediately, optionally up to SoC or energy limit
| PV charging
| with surplus, optionally with minimum current and minimum SoC; switch on from 1,500 W surplus after 30 seconds, switch off after 60 seconds (defaults)
| Eco charging
| fully at a low price, otherwise like PV charging
| Target charging
| Target SoC or energy amount by time
| Time charging
| in fixed time windows
| Bidirectional charging
| provided for in the code
| Stop
| no charging
|===

The modes are set per vehicle via “charging profiles”. Phase switching with delay (default 7 minutes).

[#sec-load-management]
== Load management

[#sec-openems-2]
=== OpenEMS

* **EVSE cluster:** distributes surplus to several charge points (even or by priority). According to the code, it has no limit for the house connection.
* **Multi-charging point management** (older connection): protects the grid connection; limit per phase, minimum power per car (default 4,500 W), order of charging stations, optionally free discharge power of the storage. Details on xref:user/devices/cluster.adoc[Cluster].
* Limits of wallbox and vehicle (minimum/maximum power single- and three-phase) are taken into account in EVSE control.

[#sec-evcc-3]
=== evcc

* **Circuits**: Each circuit has a maximum current and/or maximum power, optionally a meter and a higher-level circuit. This allows house connection, sub-distribution and individual lines to be modeled. Limits can also come dynamically from outside (e.g. from the grid operator).
* Charge points are given a **priority**.

[#sec-openwb-3]
=== openWB

* **Meter hierarchy**: Each meter (utility meter, intermediate meter) has current limits per phase (default 35 A) and optionally a power limit; the charge points are located below it.
* **Unbalanced load limitation** adjustable.
* If the meter fails, a substitute power applies (default 7,000 W).

[#sec-strengths-and-limits]
== Strengths and limits

[cols="3*",options="header"]
|===
| 
| Strengths
| Limits

| OpenEMS
| one system for storage, PV, heat and charging; joint planning by the energy optimizer; gentle control with lock times; runs on the energy manager itself
| fewer wallboxes; no vehicle SoC; load management for the house connection only with the older connection; two parallel charge point connections
| evcc
| very many wallboxes and vehicles; flexible storage rules; circuits; charging plans with prices
| needs its own device; only controls the storage via its interface; with OpenEMS only coarse storage control (hold/charge)
| openWB
| everything in the wallbox; well-thought-out load management with meter hierarchy and unbalanced load; many charging modes
| essentially only openWB hardware; read-only with OpenEMS
|===

[#sec-which-combination]
== Which combination?

* **OpenEMS only:** if the wallbox is supported by OpenEMS (preferably EVSE connection) and no vehicle SoC is required.
* **OpenEMS {plus} evcc:** if the wallbox is only supported by evcc or vehicle SoC and charging plans are important. In this case, do not additionally set up surplus charging in OpenEMS for this wallbox, and determine who controls the storage.
* **OpenEMS {plus} openWB:** openWB controls itself and reads the values from OpenEMS. OpenEMS can only display the openWB. For storage rules in openWB, note that openWB cannot control the OpenEMS storage.
* The following applies in every combination: **only one system controls the same wallbox**, and OpenEMS control functions (e.g. time-of-use tariff) can interact with external specifications (see xref:user/third-party/overview.adoc[Third-party systems on OpenEMS]).
