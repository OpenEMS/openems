The FENECON Home is a home storage system consisting of a hybrid inverter, battery and energy management. This page summarizes what the **OpenEMS source code** contains about the FENECON Home: structure, settings, protective functions and additional functions. FENECON devices run the manufacturer's own FEMS; individual functions may differ there or be available earlier.

Technically, the inverters come from the GoodWe family. In the code, the series are called “ET” (Home 10), “ETT – Home Series 20 & 30 kW” and “EUB – Home Gen2 Series 6, 10 & 15 kW”. The inverter type is recognized by the serial number. The Home apps may only be seen and deleted by installers.

**DC inputs on Home 20 and 30:** Previously, the individual PV strings were entered there (Home 20: 4 strings, Home 30: 6 strings, i.e. 2 strings per MPPT). This division is marked as “no longer supported” in the code and is only displayed for existing systems. New entries are made per MPPT input: 2 for the Home 20, 3 for the Home 30.

[#sec-impermissible-combination]
=== Impermissible combination

OpenEMS checks whether the inverter and battery modules match. If the combination is not permitted, for example a Home 10 inverter (Gen 1) with 2.8 kWh modules or a Home 20/30 with 2.2 kWh modules, then:

* **charging and discharging are blocked** (battery current 0 A),
* the warning appears: “The installed inverter and battery combination is not authorised. Operation could cause hardware damages, so charging and discharging is blocked. Please install a complete Home 10, Home 20 or Home 30 system.”

OpenEMS reads the module type from the battery management. With very old battery firmware that does not report the type, 52 Ah (2.2 kWh) is assumed.

[#sec-control-computer-generations-do-not-confuse]
=== Control computer generations (do not confuse)

In the code, “Gen2” and “Gen3” also appear for the **control computer** (compute module “CM4S Gen2” or “CM4S Gen3”). This is independent of the Home generation. The following depend on it, for example:

* the relay used to start the battery (`+io0/Relay6+` for CM4S Gen3, otherwise `+io0/Relay4+`),
* whether an additional relay board is set up,
* the status LED and the § 14a limitation.

[#sec-country-and-grid]
=== Country and grid

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===

[#sec-feed-in]
=== Feed-in

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| Type of limitation
| Meaning

| No limitation
| no limitation of feed-in
| Dynamic feed-in
| The feed-in at the grid connection is limited to a fixed value (e.g. 60 % or 70 % of the PV power); OpenEMS controls the inverter for this.
| Ripple control receiver

| Dynamic ripple control receiver
| Ripple control receiver whose specifications OpenEMS implements dynamically
| Dynamic feed-in & ripple control receiver

The maximum feed-in power is stored centrally in the system settings and can also be changed there later (“Feed-In Settings”). This value is additionally limited by the fusing of the grid connection.

[#sec-meters]
=== Meters

* **Grid meter:** Home 3-phase sensor with 120 A current transformer (default) or without current transformer. On Gen2 models, the sensor is integrated; an external one is optional. With current transformers, the primary current is entered (200 to 5000 A, secondary 5 A).
* **AC meter** (Home 10, 20, 30): optional meter for an additional AC-coupled PV system (Socomec or KDK type).

[#sec-pv]
=== PV

* **DC PV inputs:** Which inputs (MPPT) are occupied; each can be given its own name.
* **Shadow management:** The inverter regularly searches for the best operating point when modules are partially shaded. **It only has to be deactivated if power optimizers are installed on the modules.**

[#sec-emergency-power]
=== Emergency power

* **Activate emergency power supply**: whether an emergency power output is available.
* **Emergency reserve**: Part of the battery is not discharged during normal operation so that it is available in the event of a power failure. Adjustable from 5 to 100 %, default 10 %.

[#sec-other]
=== Other

* **§ 14a EnWG:** If a control device of the grid operator is connected, the storage power can be limited on its instructions (depending on the hardware).

In detail on the battery (BMS communication, charging and discharging limits, emergency reserve, startup and shutdown): xref:user/systems/fenecon-home-battery.adoc[FENECON Home battery].

[#sec-protective-functions]
=== Protective functions

* **Charging and discharging current** are limited depending on cell voltage and temperature. After a limitation, the current is only increased slowly again (0.1 A per second).
* **Deep discharge protection:** If the cell voltage falls too low (critical limit 2.8 V), OpenEMS reports in stages (see table).
* At very low state of charge, the battery can be force-charged with a small current (2 A per tower).

.Messages at low cell voltage
[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| Level
| Message

| Warning
| “Low battery state of charge because the battery cannot be charged by the inverter. Without charging, the battery will soon switch off to protect itself.”
| Fault
| “Low state of charge. The battery will soon switch off to protect itself.”
| Fault
| “Battery was switched off due to too low a state of charge. Please contact your installer.”
|===

[#sec-startup-updates-extension]
=== Startup, updates, extension

* The battery is switched on at startup via a relay (`+io0/Relay4+`, with control computer CM4S Gen3 `+io0/Relay6+`).
* OpenEMS contains a function to update the battery **firmware**.
* **Extending the battery:** An auxiliary function (“Prepare Battery Extension”) brings the storage to a suitable state of charge before installing further modules.
* **Measuring the state of health (SoH):** An optional function (“SoH Cycle”) carries out a charge/discharge cycle and uses it to determine the state of health of the battery.

[#sec-inverter]
== Inverter

[#sec-control-by-openems]
=== Control by OpenEMS

The inverter can be operated in three ways:

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| Mode
| Meaning

| SMART (default)
| The inverter controls itself (fast); OpenEMS switches to its own specifications when required, e.g. for price control or grid charging.
| INTERNAL
| Only the internal control of the inverter; no specifications from OpenEMS.
| REMOTE
| OpenEMS specifies everything. Slower, but possible without a smart meter at the grid connection.
|===

[#sec-grid-regulations]
=== Grid regulations

The inverter component contains extensive settings for the grid connection rules, including grid code (VDE-AR-N 4105 / 4110), power reduction at over- and underfrequency (P(F)), power depending on voltage (P(U)) and reactive power methods (fixed cos φ, cos φ(P), Q(U), Q(P)). The installer sets these values according to the specifications of the grid operator.

[#sec-emergency-power-2]
=== Emergency power

* The **emergency power output** can be switched on or off (“Off-Grid outlet”).
* A separate meter (`+meter2+`) measures the power at the emergency power output.
* In island mode, OpenEMS shows the grid state “Off-Grid” and counts the time in island mode.
* There is a connection via an STS box for emergency generators.

[#sec-dc-surplus]
=== DC surplus

If the PV modules on the inverter supply more than the house and battery can absorb (e.g. battery full), the “Surplus-Feed-To-Grid” function ensures that this DC surplus is fed into the grid and not curtailed, provided the feed-in limit allows it.

[#sec-status-led]
== Status LED

OpenEMS controls a status LED (component “System FENECON Home”, `+system0+`). It is set up automatically by the apps for **Home 6, Home 10 Gen2, Home 15, Home 20 and Home 30**, and also by the Commercial systems. **On the Home 10 (Gen 1), the app does not set up a status LED.** A further prerequisite is a control computer with its own switching outputs (CM3, CM4, CM4S, CM4S Gen2 or Gen3). Only an admin can set up the LED app manually.

The LED shows:

[cols="2*",options="header"]
|===
| LED
| Meaning (according to the code)

| blue, continuous
| no fault and no warning
| blue, flashing (1 second)
| warning or fault, and no connection to the backend
| off
| warning or fault with an existing connection to the backend
|===

The LED also has red and green contacts; however, only blue is used for the status in the current code.

[#sec-control-computer]
== Control computer

The FENECON Home contains a control computer based on a Raspberry Pi Compute Module. The code distinguishes between CM3, CM4, CM4S, CM4S Gen2 and Gen3. Some functions (e.g. status LED, § 14a limitation) depend on this hardware.

[#sec-what-else-openems-does-with-the-home]
== What else OpenEMS does with the Home

All general functions described on the other pages apply to the Home:

* xref:user/basics/energy-flows.adoc[Energy flows and total values]: Grid meter, DC PV inputs and storage supply the values for `+_sum+`.
* xref:user/storage-control/grid-optimized-charge.adoc[Grid-optimized charge] and xref:user/storage-control/time-of-use-tariffs.adoc[time-of-use tariffs] control the storage.
* xref:user/ev-charging-control/surplus-charging.adoc[Surplus charging] and xref:user/io-control/heat-pumps-and-heating-elements.adoc[heat pump/heating element] use the PV surplus.
* The xref:user/data/history-rrd4j.adoc[history] is stored locally; the data is optionally transferred to a xref:user/cloud-interfaces/backend-data-transfer.adoc[backend].

[#sec-good-to-know]
== Good to know

* This page describes what is available in the **OpenEMS source code** for the FENECON Home. It does not say whether and how a FENECON Home can be operated with plain OpenEMS. FENECON ships the devices with its own FEMS.
* Many settings (country setting, grid regulations, feed-in limitation) are **legally relevant** and belong in the hands of the installer.
* Only deactivate shadow management if power optimizers are installed.
